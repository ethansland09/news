Scarlett Johansson to buy Samsung Mobile for $1 billion — and relaunch it as Scarlet

The proposed mobile reboot would put customization, more powerful chips and a radically personal Android experience at the center of a new smartphone brand.
The Signal Technology Desk  ·  6 min read  ·  November 9 special
A new name. A new mobile identity.

A billion-dollar reboot built around the user

In this imagined business scenario, Scarlett Johansson's proposed $1 billion purchase would turn Samsung's mobile division into an independent consumer technology company called Scarlet. The plan would go beyond a logo change: Scarlet would aim to make phones feel more personal, configurable and responsive to the way each owner actually uses a device.

The concept centers on Samsung's mobile hardware and software operations, rather than the entire Samsung Group. Displays, memory, processors and other components could continue to come from established suppliers and technology partners while Scarlet develops its own product identity.

The Scarlet promise: make the phone yours

Customization would be the headline feature. Owners could reshape the interface with adjustable themes, icon systems, fonts, animations, lock screens, always-on displays and interactive widgets. A built-in theme studio would let users create and share complete visual styles without relying on third-party launchers.

Better chips, not just bigger benchmark numbers

The proposed engineering strategy would prioritize custom mobile processors with stronger sustained performance, lower power draw and improved graphics. Rather than chasing short benchmark bursts, the goal would be a phone that stays responsive during long gaming sessions, video editing and demanding multitasking.

Scarlet could begin with a co-designed processor from an established semiconductor partner, then move toward more customized silicon as the business matured. That would be a more practical first step than attempting to create a cutting-edge chip operation from scratch.

“The phone should adapt to the person — not force the person to adapt to the phone.”
— Imagined Scarlet product philosophy

Can Scarlet compete?

The opportunity would be differentiation. Premium phones already offer excellent cameras and fast displays; Scarlet's pitch would combine polished hardware, unusually deep personalization and a flexible software experience that remains easy to use.

The challenge would be execution: securing component supply, supporting millions of devices, maintaining Android compatibility and earning customer trust. A $1 billion purchase price would only be the beginning of the investment required to build a global brand.

What could come next?

The first product reveal in this concept would introduce the Scarlet One flagship, followed by a more affordable Scarlet Core. Both would emphasize customizable themes, high-end displays, strong cameras and extended software support.
